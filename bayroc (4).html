BayROC

Bay Area Recycling Outreach Coalition · "Reuse to Go" campaign

Project Manager · with Ruhee Bhatnagar & Navya Mishra

The client

BayROC is a coalition of Bay Area cities working under one message to reduce single-use waste — over 50% of single-use food waste ends up in landfill. Their existing "Reuse to Go" program encouraged people to bring reusable containers, but it needed real engagement behind it.

Click through the full deck

The challenge

BayROC's existing marketing wasn't landing with younger audiences, and worse, it had no way to actually measure success — metrics were all views, with nothing tracking real-world reuse behavior. On top of that, reuse itself was perceived as socially awkward or inconvenient, and participating restaurants had almost no visibility for their reuse incentives.

Target market

Primary audience: young adults 16–30, mostly students and early-career professionals across the SF Bay Area — environmentally aware, but convenience-driven and heavily influenced by peers and social media. Secondary audience: cafés and fast-casual restaurants, small-to-medium Bay Area businesses that were open to sustainability but needed low-cost visibility.

My role

As Project Manager, I owned strategy execution, quality oversight, and performance tracking across the whole campaign, while Ruhee led campaign research and outreach coordination and Navya led social media strategy and creative direction.

The plan

We built the campaign around a framework I'll admit I'm proud of: R.E.U.S.E. — Reach the Right Audience (TikTok and Instagram outreach, +30% video completion), Encourage Behavior Change (drive QR pledge participation), Uplift Reuse Norms (peer-led content to make reuse feel normal instead of awkward), Set Measurable Metrics (QR-based tracking tied to real behavior, not just views), and Engagement (amplify existing reward programs and promote participating businesses).

The 10-week schedule moved through four milestones: research and identifying barriers (weeks 1–2), a strategy redesign around youth targeting (weeks 3–5), an incentive model built on restaurant and reward partnerships (weeks 6–8), and a final evaluation (weeks 9–10). Total campaign budget was $3,475, with the majority going to digital outreach.

Execution

We launched Gen Z–focused content on Instagram, shifted to short-form trend-based visuals to boost engagement, and reframed reuse around rewards instead of obligation. QR pledge stations went up at partner restaurants, backed by table signage and flyers, so we could measure actual commitment instead of impressions.

Staying on track

Content production hit timing bottlenecks early on, solved by streamlining approvals and moving to a structured content calendar. Restaurant partners were slow to respond, so we set clear response timelines and prioritized the partners who were ready to move. When audiences seemed confused about what "reuse" actually required, we created instructional visuals and step-by-step guidance instead of assuming it was self-explanatory.

Lessons learned

The youth-focused platform shift, QR-based behavior tracking, and reward-driven messaging all worked. What didn't work as well at first was QR participation — low in the early weeks because messaging prioritized awareness over action, and because reuse still felt socially awkward to some. If I ran this again, I'd launch restaurant partnerships earlier, strengthen the in-store call-to-action, and lean harder into influencer collaboration from day one instead of building up to it.
