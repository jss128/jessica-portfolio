SkillSpark

De Anza College · Dual-Enrollment UI/UX · Final Case Study, March 28, 2025

SkillSpark is a gamified skill-building app concept, designed to help students find the motivation to learn new things. The idea: small, engaging lessons with quizzes, leaderboards, and streaks, plus the option to pair up with a friend and learn the same topic together — so skill-building feels less like a chore and more like something you actually stick with.

The problem

The app was built to address three things: a lack of motivation among high schoolers juggling school and work, the difficulty of exploring new interests without committing to a long class, and the reality that a lot of students don't know what they want to pursue yet and just want an easy way to try things out.

Research & insights

I started with a user persona to ground the design in an actual person's problem instead of an abstract user. Erica Johnson, 17, is a junior in San Jose who also works as a swim coach. Her core needs were good grades without sacrificing her job, and a study tool that could actually hold her attention. Her frustrations: staying motivated, not having enough time to study efficiently, and disengaging from long, comprehensive videos.

That research pointed to four key findings: motivation is a major struggle, time efficiency is crucial, short attention spans need interactive learning, and personalization increases engagement. Those four findings became the design brief I built everything else against — gamification, efficiency, and interactivity.

Ideation

I mapped the app out with a mindmap first — the problem (students who are weak in skills, unmotivated, and short on time), the audience (teens and busy individuals who want to learn something new), and the solution (customizable skill paths, gamification, social features). From there I brainstormed specific ways to make skill-building feel fun rather than another obligation: XP for completing tasks, streaks, badges, and a "build your own skill" flow through interactive quizzes.

Task flows

I mapped task flows for two personas — a busy student enrolling in a course, and a less-motivated student challenging a friend — to make sure every flow tied back to an actual need. For Anna (busy, wants to learn efficiently), the flow was built around getting from "open the app" to "start first lesson" in as few steps as possible. For Erica, the flow leaned into the social side: pick a friend, pick a challenge, compete on a shared leaderboard.

Wireframes & iteration

I sketched low- and mid-fidelity wireframes to work out the user journey before touching high fidelity. My first pass at low-fidelity was actually too detailed, which made it harder to iterate — a good early lesson in leaving room to change things. Based on that, I refined navigation, filtering, and how goal-setting worked when a user enrolled in a course.

Style guide

For the visual system, I started with a moodboard to find a look that felt dreamy but still sleek. Typography landed on Poppins with a Major Third (1.25) type scale, chosen for a balance of clarity and visual appeal — the scale creates clear visual separation between heading levels so navigation stays intuitive without feeling cluttered.

Color took more iteration. I originally defined a monochrome palette, then tested triadic, analogous, and complementary schemes in Adobe Color. The analogous palette matched my mood board best, but it failed accessibility contrast checks and couldn't be fixed without breaking the analogous relationship. I ended up increasing contrast and adjusting the palette to keep the same dreamy, game-like feeling while passing accessibility.

High-fidelity design

The final screens cover the core loop: explore skills by category, preview a course (difficulty, rating, suggested frequency), and enroll. I designed a custom icon set for the navigation bar specifically so the colors stayed consistent across every screen, rather than relying on a generic icon pack that wouldn't match the palette.

Reflection

The biggest challenge was keeping the app both engaging and easy to navigate at the same time — those two goals pull against each other more than I expected. Multiple iterations and a closer look at user feedback helped me refine the task flows and UI components into something more seamless. The main lesson was learning to balance aesthetics with functionality and let research actually drive decisions instead of just instinct. If I extended this project, I'd run real usability testing and personalize the experience further based on how each student actually learns.

SkillSpark was designed solo, end-to-end — persona research, ideation, task flows, wireframes, a full style guide, and final high-fidelity screens — for a De Anza College dual-enrollment UI/UX course.
